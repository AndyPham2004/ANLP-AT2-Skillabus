We put subject outlines, job ads and resumes into one skills vocabulary, so a student can see exactly which skills a role needs that they lack, and which subject fills each gap.

1The problem

Subject outlines speak academic. Job ads speak industry. Nothing maps one to the other, so students guess which electives to take and what to put on a resume.

"Apply and evaluate machine learning algorithms on real data and communicate the results." UTS Handbook, subject learning objective (representative wording)
"Proven experience building and deploying machine learning models in production on AWS or Azure." LinkedIn ad, Data Scientist, Sydney

Same skill, different words, and only the ad asks for cloud. The app makes that difference visible. It takes the teaching team's study planner idea from the timetable to the job market.

2Users and goals

Elective chooserWhich subject moves me closest to the role I want?
Job seekerWhat does my resume lack for this ad?
Career switcherHow far is my profile from a new role?
  • Upload a resume, or tick the subjects you have taken
  • Pick a target role and city
  • See your skill gap ranked by demand
  • Get electives ranked by how much gap they close
  • Ask a chat that answers from real ads

Ethics

Resumes stay in memory, never on disk. Names, emails and phone numbers are masked before any LLM call. Ads are used under the dataset licence. Results are labelled as suggestions, not career advice.

4Pipeline

  1. Inputsubject outline, job ad or resume; PDF resumes parsed with pypdf
  2. Cleandeduplicate ads, strip boilerplate, split sentences
  3. Extract skillsthree ways, compared: ESCO dictionary and regex, spaCy NER, LLM
  4. Normalisemap aliases to one ESCO id, so "ML" and "machine learning" agree
  5. Matchtwo ways, compared: TF-IDF cosine versus sentence embeddings
  6. Outputgap chart, ranked electives, demand trend, RAG chat over ads

Copper steps are where a traditional and a newer method run side by side and are scored (block 8).
